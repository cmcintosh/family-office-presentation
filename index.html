What's a "Trust"?

A trust is just a safe box with rules.

🏠 The house goes in the box.

📋 The rules say: "This house belongs to the McIntosh family. It can't be taken away. It can't be sold to a stranger. It stays in the family forever."

🛡️ The box is protected — not even Medicaid or Medicare can take the house to pay for medical care.

👨‍👩‍👦 I manage the box. When I'm gone, someone else in the family takes over.

That's it. No magic, no tricks. Just a legal way to keep things safe — even if health costs pile up later in life.

🏡

🚨

Your house becomes the foundation that holds up the whole family.

Questions You Might Have

Will I lose my house?
No. You keep living there. The trust owns it on paper, but it's still your home in real life. Nobody is kicking you out.
Can someone take it from me?
No. That's the whole point of the trust. Once it's in there, no outside person, no lawsuit, no creditor can touch it. It's locked in for the family.
What about Dakota?
Dakota contributes $500/month toward the house. That money goes toward loan payments and keeping the home in good shape — building long-term value for the family. Dakota's living arrangements don't change. This is about making the house work for everyone.

More Questions

What if I want to move?
You can. If you ever want to downsize or move to a smaller place, we sell the house and the money goes back into the family trust to use for whatever you need next.
What if I need Medicaid or Medicare help later?
This is one of the biggest reasons to do this. Once the house is in the trust, it's protected. If you ever need Medicaid for long-term care or nursing home support, they can't take the house or force it to be sold to pay them back. The trust keeps it safe for the family no matter what happens with your health.
What happens when I'm gone?
The house stays in the trust. It doesn't go through probate court. It doesn't get taxed as an inheritance. It just stays in the family, exactly like we set it up.
